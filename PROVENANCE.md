# Image provenance

Every image the site ships, and where it came from. Generated and checked
against `flirt-site/scripts/import-media.mjs`, which is the only thing that
writes into `public/images`.

**There is no stock photography on this site.** Not one image came from Getty,
Shutterstock, Adobe Stock, Unsplash, Pexels, or any other library. So the
specific risk — using licensed stock without a licence — does not currently
exist. Brooklyn said stock is fine for the remaining gaps, but none has been
added yet. If it is, see "Adding stock safely" at the bottom.

That is not the same as saying every image is definitely cleared. Read on.

## The 39 from the old site — the part worth checking

These were on `flirtlashlounge.com`, which was Brooklyn's site. But *"it was on
her website"* is not the same as *"she owns the copyright."* Three distinct
things could be true of any given file:

**1. Photos she or her team shot.** Almost certainly fine.

**2. Photos a hired photographer shot.** The salon and team images
(`team-group.jpg`, `team-group-2.jpg`, `training-graduates.jpg`,
`hero-lounge.jpg` — a 3840×5760 DSLR file) look like a professional shoot.
Under US law the photographer owns the copyright unless the contract says
otherwise or it was work-for-hire. A commissioning client very often has
permission to use the images and still does not own them. **Ask Brooklyn who
shot these and what the contract said.** This is the single most likely place
a complaint would come from, and it is cheap to resolve.

**3. Supplier product photography.** These look like manufacturer images from
the old Wix shop, not photos she took:

- `product-candle-luxe.jpg`, `product-candle-cozy.jpg` — branded "AURA CANDLES"
- `product-jars.jpg`, `product-adhesive.jpg`, `product-lashes.jpg`,
  `product-tweezers.jpg`

Suppliers usually provide these for resellers to use, but that is a permission
worth confirming rather than assuming.

**Good news:** all six product images, plus twelve others, are currently
**not displayed anywhere on the site** — 40 of the 58 are referenced, the rest
are unused files sitting in `public/`. Deleting the unused ones removes that
question entirely. See the list at the end.

## A separate issue: the people in the photographs

This is not copyright, it is likeness and consent, and it is worth separating:

- `training-graduates.jpg` shows **four students' full names legibly** on their
  certificates. Already flagged in `NOTES.md`.
- Several images show identifiable guests mid-treatment.
- The team photos show identifiable staff, some of whom may no longer work
  there.

For a business publishing photos of its own clients and staff, the normal fix
is a release — or simply asking. Worth a pass before launch.

## What I deliberately did not use

The Pinterest reference images Brooklyn sent (the SOLÉA STUDIO board, the
Coastal Cove palette, the chiffon and plaster textures) are **not on the site**
and never were — I verified this by comparing file sizes against the build.
She sent them as a colour and texture reference, and that is all they were used
for. Publishing them would have been an actual copyright problem: they are
someone else's brand identity work and stock photography, lifted to Pinterest.

The soft washes in the background of the site are CSS gradients I wrote, not
images, precisely so nothing needed licensing.

## Adding stock safely

If you do add stock, these are free for commercial use with no attribution
required and no licence to buy:

- **Unsplash** — unsplash.com/license. Free commercial use. Cannot be resold as
  stock itself. Note it does *not* grant model or property releases, so avoid
  recognisable faces for a commercial beauty context.
- **Pexels** — pexels.com/license. Same shape.
- **Pixabay** — pixabay.com/service/license-summary.

Paid, and **not** to be used without buying a licence: Getty, Shutterstock,
Adobe Stock, iStock, Depositphotos. Getty in particular runs automated reverse
image searches and sends demand letters — that is the exact scenario to avoid.

Whatever is added, record the source URL and licence next to it in
`scripts/import-media.mjs`, the way the recovered images are recorded now.
